Research

Precision Motion Control and Mechatronics

Rapid axis motion excites the structural dynamics of machine tools and other precision motion systems, leaving residual vibration after each move and cross-talk between axes. Both cause longer dwell times and limit productivity. I model and identify the machine's dynamics, and develop data-driven feedback and feedforward controllers that suppress these trajectory-induced vibrations through active dampers and the machine's existing feed drives, for faster settling and higher accuracy.

Active damper control

Auto-tuned feedback

The velocity-feedback law tunes itself from the measured vibration response by solving a constrained quadratic program each time the machine repeats a task.

Model-based iterative feedback tuning (IFT) In preparation
70% less damper stroke (smaller damper) up to 28% faster settling to 2 µm

Learned feedforward

The feedforward control signal is tuned iteratively to suppress the residual vibration that feedback alone cannot remove.

Iterative learning control (ILC) CIRP Annals
90% lower peak-to-peak error 98% faster settling to 2 µm (29.7 → 0.6 ms)

Cross-axis vibration compensation through the feed drives

A trajectory pre-filter adds a small Z-axis command that cancels the tool-tip vibration excited by rapid Y-axis motion, and is fine-tuned through machine-in-the-loop iterations.

Trajectory pre-filtering ICPE 2024
77% lower peak vibration 5.5 → 1.3 µm

Machining Process Control

Machining disturbs the machine tool in several ways, such as forced and chatter vibration from the cutting forces, uneven tooth loads from tool runout, and spindle speed ripple from the cutting torque. I model the kinematics and dynamics of the machining process, and build adaptive controllers that suppress these process-induced disturbances through the spindle and feed drives, for higher accuracy and productivity.

Adaptive spindle speed control

A model-free extremum-seeking controller tunes the spindle speed from vibration feedback during milling, to suppress chatter and forced vibrations.

Extremum-seeking control (ESC) CIRP JMST ASME MSEC
85% lower RMS vibration 131 → 20 m/s²

Adaptive tool runout compensation through the feed drives

A micro-circular feed motion cancels the effect of tool runout so that every tooth cuts the same chip, and is tuned online from force or acceleration feedback.

Convex optimization ASME JMSE Patent pending
86% lower force variation 101 → 14 N

Real-time chatter detection

Lightweight algorithms flag chatter onset from the vibration signal in real time, and are light enough to run on embedded hardware.

Spectral and principal component analysis (PCA) indicators Manufacturing Letters
Real-time detection at chatter onset flagged when the power ratio crosses 50%

Spindle speed ripple compensation through the spindle drive

Sinusoidal speed commands at the tooth-passing frequency and its harmonics cancel the speed ripple caused by cutting torque.
