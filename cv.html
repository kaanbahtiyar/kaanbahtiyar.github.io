Summary

I combine dynamic modeling, system identification and real-time optimization to develop learning-based and adaptive controllers that suppress trajectory-induced vibrations and process-induced disturbances in machine tools and motion stages, and I validate them on real hardware. Author of publications in CIRP and ASME journals, presenter at ASME and ASPE meetings, and co-inventor on a pending patent.

Experience

Graduate Research Assistant, Oregon State University 2020-Present
  • Developed learning-based feedforward control for an active inertial damper, reducing peak positioning error 87% (12 to 1.6 µm) on a feed-drive testbed within actuator stroke and force limits. An auto-tuned feedback law cut damper stroke 70% against conventional tuning.
  • Characterized cross-axis coupling on a 3-axis machine tool and built trajectory pre-filtering that cut axis-coupled Z vibration 80% (6 to 1.2 µm).
  • Developed a model-free adaptive spindle speed controller driven by real-time vibration feedback, eliminating chatter with up to 85% lower RMS vibration and 57 to 66% lower vibration than conventional chatter-frequency control.
  • Developed adaptive tool runout compensation through the feed drives, cutting peak cutting-force variation 86% (101 to 14 N) and supporting a pending patent.
  • Developed lightweight, data-driven algorithms that detect chatter onset in real time.
  • Trained and mentored graduate and undergraduate students; guided two M.Sc. theses.
Graduate Teaching Assistant, Oregon State University 2020-Present
