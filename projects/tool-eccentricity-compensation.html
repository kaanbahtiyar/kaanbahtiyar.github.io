Projects / Tool runout compensation

Tool runout compensation

Tool runout offsets the cutter's center from its axis of rotation, so each tooth cuts a different chip. That uneven load roughens the surface and wears the most-loaded tooth first. I cancel it with the machine's own feed drives, with no added hardware.

Convex optimization ASME JMSE MM Science Patent pending

Method

Runout makes the tool center trace a small circle at the spindle frequency. The compensation commands the opposite micro-circular trajectory through the X and Y feed drives, injected into the 5 kHz servo position loop on top of the nominal toolpath.

Feed-drive dynamics distort the injected motion, so its amplitude and phase are tuned on the machine from force or accelerometer feedback, by minimizing the spindle-synchronous content that runout produces. The tuning is a convex optimization with Newton-type updates, so the optimum is unique and convergence is fast and repeatable.

Results

86% lower peak cutting-force variation 101 → 14 N
2 to 3 updates to converge 10 to 15 spindle revolutions

Measured in steel-alloy milling on a 3-axis CNC, and validated with 2-, 3- and 4-tooth cutters across spindle speeds. An accelerometer-only variant also converges, in about 10 iterations.
