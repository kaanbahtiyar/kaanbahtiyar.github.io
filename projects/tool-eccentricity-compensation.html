Tool runout compensation

Tool runout is an offset between the cutter's center and its axis of rotation, so each tooth cuts a different amount of chip. This uneven load roughens the surface and wears the most heavily loaded tooth first. I developed an adaptive strategy that compensates for the runout using the machine's own feed drives, with no additional hardware.

Convex optimization ASME JMSE MM Science Patent pending

Method

Runout makes the tool center trace a small circle around the spindle axis at the spindle frequency. The proposed compensation adds a counteracting micro-circular trajectory to the nominal toolpath, commanded through the X and Y feed drives in the 5 kHz position loop.

The feed-drive dynamics distort the injected motion, and the runout itself (its magnitude and its angular position on the spindle) is unknown. The amplitude and phase of the compensation are therefore tuned on the machine, using force or accelerometer measurements, by minimizing the spindle-frequency component that runout produces. The tuning problem is convex and is solved with Newton-type updates, so it has a single optimum and converges quickly and repeatably.

Results

86% lower peak cutting-force variation 101 → 14 N
2 to 3 updates to converge 10 to 15 spindle revolutions

Measured in steel-alloy milling on a 3-axis CNC, and validated with 2-, 3- and 4-tooth cutters across spindle speeds.
