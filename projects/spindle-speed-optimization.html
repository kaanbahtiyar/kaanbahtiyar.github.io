Adaptive spindle speed control

Vibration in milling appears in two forms: chatter, an instability that can scrap the part and damage the tool, and forced vibration, which is stable but still degrades surface quality. I developed a real-time spindle speed controller based on extremum-seeking control that suppresses both during machining, without a process model.

Extremum seeking CIRP JMST ASME MSEC

Method

Spindle speed selection runs as an online optimization during the cut. A vibration cost is computed from accelerometer data once per spindle revolution, and an extremum-seeking controller moves the speed in the direction that lowers it. It needs no process model, stability lobes or modal testing.

The controller minimizes total vibration, forced and chatter, and adjusts the spindle speed through the CNC's standard spindle-override input via its PLC.

Results

85% lower RMS vibration 131 → 20 m/s²
57 to 66% lower than the conventional chatter mitigation strategy

Measured in steel-alloy milling on a 3-axis CNC: Starting from an already stable cut, the controller still halved RMS vibration (8 → 4 m/s²).
