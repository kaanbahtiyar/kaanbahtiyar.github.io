Projects / Chatter detection and adaptive spindle speed control

Chatter detection and adaptive spindle speed control

Two separate methods for milling vibration: a model-free controller that tunes the spindle speed while the machine cuts, and lightweight algorithms that detect chatter in real time.

Extremum seeking CIRP JMST Manufacturing Letters

Adaptive spindle speed control

Spindle speed selection runs as an online optimization during the cut. A vibration cost is computed from accelerometer data once per spindle revolution, and an extremum-seeking controller moves the speed in the direction that lowers it. It needs no process model, stability lobes or modal testing.

The controller minimizes total vibration, forced plus chatter, and talks to a production CNC through the standard spindle-override input.

85% lower RMS vibration 131 → 20 m/s²
57 to 66% lower than the conventional chatter-frequency strategy

Measured in steel-alloy milling on a 3-axis CNC. Starting from an already stable cut, the controller still halved RMS vibration (8 → 4 m/s²).

Chatter detection

Two indicators flag chatter onset from the vibration signal. The first compares total signal power with the power at the tooth-passing harmonics, and rises when broadband chatter energy appears between them. The second applies principal component analysis to a windowed data matrix, and is more robust to changes in tooling and cutting conditions.

Both are light enough to run in real time on embedded hardware. On a tapered workpiece, they stay low during stable cutting and rise clearly at chatter onset.

Testbed

3-axis CNC machine tool, accelerometers, dynamometer, spindle override via PLC, 10 kHz data acquisition, dSPACE real-time I/O, MATLAB/Simulink real-time.
