Real-time chatter detection

Chatter is a self-excited instability that grows fast in milling, marks the part and damage the equipment. Detecting it early is the first step of any countermeasure. I developed two lightweight indicators that flag its onset in real time.

Spectral Analysis Principal Component Analysis (PCA) Manufacturing Letters NAMRC 50

Method

A simple amplitude threshold is unreliable: forced vibration at the tooth-passing frequency masks the chatter component, and the threshold must be readjusted for each cutting condition. I developed indicators that separate chatter from forced vibration, so a single ratio-based threshold works across cutting conditions.

The first compares the total signal power with the power at the tooth-passing harmonics in the frequency domain, and it rises when chatter energy appears. The second applies principal component analysis to a sliding window of vibration data and is more robust to changes in tooling and cutting conditions. When implemented recursively, both detect chatter in real time.

Results

On a tapered workpiece, the depth of cut grows along the path, so the cut starts stable, with forced vibration only, and turns into chatter partway through. Both indicators stay low during the stable part and cross the 50% threshold as chatter sets in.

The spectral method is the cheapest to compute, and the PCA method trades a little computation for robustness.
