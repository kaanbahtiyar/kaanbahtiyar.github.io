Projects / Active damper control

Active damper control

Fast point-to-point moves excite a machine's structural modes and leave the table ringing. An active inertial damper on the structure suppresses that vibration. I control it with auto-tuned feedback and a learned feedforward force, within the actuator's stroke and force limits.

Constrained QP Iterative learning CIRP Annals Dow Scholarship

Auto-tuned feedback

Direct velocity feedback (DVF) damps the vibration after it appears, and its tuning decides how much stroke the damper spends doing it. Here the feedback law tunes itself online: at every time step it solves a band-selective constrained optimization from the measured vibration.

70% less damper stroke
Up to 25% faster settling to ±2 µm

Compared with conventional DVF that uses a single band-pass filter, on the feed-drive testbed.

Learned feedforward

Feedback reacts only after vibration appears, so it cannot remove the initial overshoot. Because production moves repeat, a feedforward force profile is learned from the measured vibration over repeated runs and applied at the instant the excitation occurs. The profile is built from filtered B-splines, and the stroke and force limits are enforced inside the learning problem.

87% lower peak positioning error 12 → 1.6 µm
About 10 learning iterations
